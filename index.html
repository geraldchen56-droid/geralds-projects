Gerald Chen. Things I've built to learn, practise and write.

I'm Gerald, an IB Diploma student who likes economics and maths. I build small sites to understand things properly: how options are priced, how probability really works, and why people make irrational choices. Everything on this page is something I made, and I keep adding to it.

Ten projects, all live. Each one has a short note on why I made it and what it does. Note: All of these apps were made with AI assistance (namely Claude), where I write thousands of lines of code then get AI to go through and correct mistakes, I then go through changes and decide if I like the direction that it takes.

Built to understand things properly, in a way that rotates the frame of knowledge. Learning with you instead of at you.

This page counts visits with Vercel Web Analytics, which does not use cookies. PS: A lot of my inspiration comes from staring into my ceiling with the music on, give it a try, creativity feels rare in the age of AI.

Projects GitHub

  1. Edge Lab

    Why I built it. When getting to a higher level of anything, you cannot simply rely on talent and gut feeling to retain the excellency you had before. Not when everyone competing for the position is on your level or better, this is a way to stand out in a standardised test format. When everyone has played the game before where does your edge come from?.

    What it does. Interactive games for trading-interview prep: market making, ETF arbitrage, Bayes, Kelly sizing and a poker room, plus a Daily Gauntlet with a leaderboard.

    Open site

  2. Root T

    Why I built it. The Black-Schoels formula (BS) is one of the most intimidating formulas I have ever had the mind-cluttering pleasure to lay eyes on, in a struggle to break down and model it with comparison to the Monte-Carlo (MS) system on a European call option this was the result.

    What it does. Prices a European call option with 100,000 random simulations, then compares the result live against the Black-Scholes formula across 40 strikes and 20 maturities.

    Open site Code on GitHub

  3. The Irrational Margin

    Why I built it. After learning about the assumptions within economics regarding "rationality" my interest piqued. Why did economists assume normal civillians make perfect decisions when it rarely happens? To enquire further into this and personally document my findings I developed this blog.

    What it does. A blog of essays on behavioural economics, where psychology meets the way people spend, save and choose.

    Open site

  4. The Healing Pages

    Why I built it. As a neurodivergent individual, the expression of emotions and rash decision making has lead to undesireable outcomes in the past, not saying that I wish to never make a mistake again I want to journal the mistakes I have made, the thoughts I had before. It also is something to do late at night to "wrap the night up" and signal to myself it's time to go to sleep.

    What it does. A daily journal that tracks your streak, pages and word count, shows a calendar of what you've written, and has a recite mode for reading a page out loud.

    Open site

  5. IB Probability Lab

    Why I built it. I wanted to revise IB Maths AA probability properly, and give other students on the course something better than a page of formulas. Mainly it's formatted towards my way of learning one of the most annoying and knowledge based topics in AAHL, unlike calculus where the application of formulas will get the answer. (I will be adding more math topics one by one, starting out with the ones I consider the hardest; Vectors, Differential Equations.

    What it does. Covers every SL and HL probability (for now) topic with live models you can drag, Casio fx-CG50 and TI-84 keystrokes, and unlimited randomised practice questions with full working.

    Open site

  6. Study Tracker

    Why I built it. Personally, studying has always been a matter of discipline, making myself sit at a desk to review and memorise items. To make it both easier to get started with and be consistent I built a tracker that natively on my computer allows me to keep track of everything.

    What it does. Allows students to have a hub where everything study related combined into one spot, to ease the pain of studying.

    Open site Code on GitHub

  7. Mahjong Crash Course

    Why I built it. Since bouncing around major cities from Sichuan and Shenzhen to Sydney then to Shanghai (I don't know why my family seems to love cities with names starting with S) I've always struggled to connect with my Chinese heritage after being in Australia for 12 years. I built this as a way to combat that, to align myself in something I am very proud of which is my family and our traditions.

    What it does. Teaches Chinese and Hong Kong mahjong from scratch with a tile explorer, a hand checker that shows what you're waiting on, a solo practice table, scoring tables and a quiz.

    Open site

  8. IB Physics Nuclear Summary

    Why I built it. My nuclear physics notes were spread across several booklets and I wanted one place to revise IB Physics SL Topic 7.

    What it does. Covers mass defect, binding energy and the BEN curve with three worked calculation methods, fusion and fission, and a 15-point checklist that tracks your progress.
